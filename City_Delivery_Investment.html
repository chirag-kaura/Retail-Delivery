Hypothesis Testing & Analytical Proof

CONFIRMED VIA HISTORICAL DATA
Confirmed
H1 — Delivery Delay directly drives customer dissatisfaction
A Pearson Chi-Square test confirms that late delivery is strongly associated with 1-2 star reviews. When deliveries run late, customer dissatisfaction jumps from 10.7% to 58.2% (a 5.4x increase).
Chi-Square Statistic (χ²): 617.65 | p-value: 2.43e-136
Confirmed
H2 — Target region delivery latency is structural, not random noise
A Welch's T-Test compares target cities against São Paulo's logistics standard. Target cities average 19.85 days to deliver vs. 8.07 days in SP. This latency gap is statistically significant and represents a structural bottleneck.
Welch T-statistic: 47.50 | p-value: 0.00 (Highly Significant)
Confirmed
H3 — Major revenue is exposed to late delivery risks
Across the 5 selected pilot cities, R$ 79,699 in revenue (19.1% of total city revenue) is tied to orders arriving late. Resolving this represents clear operational ROI.
Exposed: R$ 79,699 | Impacted Customers: 548
Confirmed
H4 — Small set of hero categories anchors localized stocking
Under the Pareto Principle, we find that 5-6 core categories (Health/Beauty, Watches, Auto, Computers, Bed/Bath) represent the majority of regional sales. Pre-positioning just these categories locally resolves the transit gap.

Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) Pipeline

END-TO-END LATENCY PREDICTION
The ML Problem: We built a Binary Classification model (Random Forest) to predict if an order will be late at the moment of checkout. By looking at freight value, seller/customer state, product dimensions, and price, we can dynamically identify high-risk orders and route them differently or subsidize faster shipping, protecting our at-risk revenue.
